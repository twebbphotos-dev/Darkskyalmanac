## v5.14 (zion-sky-v483)
Lunar Rover: sudden bounces and backflips. Three faults in the contact physics. (1) Hard hits bounced back almost elastically: wheel and frame forces are capped (9000 N a strut, 14000 N the frame), and at the cap the force going in matched the force coming out, so damping did nothing. A rover hitting a crater wall at 7–8 m/s left it going up at about 5 m/s. Now the bump stop pushes back with at most 1500 N and the frame with at most 2500 N, so regolith and wire-mesh tires soak up the blow. (2) Each wheel's ground force acted along the chassis's own up, not square to the ground. A rising wall never slowed the rover, and all of the hit went into pitching it. (3) The suspension rebounded at the same light damping it compressed with, so cresting a rise the springs flung the rover off nose-down. Rebound damping is now 1600 N·s/m (compression stays 620), as on an off-road damper. In stress runs, sudden spin jumps fell from several to none and peak spin from 2.2 to 1.6 rad/s. Past about 53° of tilt the rover now goes over instead of righting itself (the old forces could hold it up at 60°). Dust: fewer, smaller, fainter grains with a softer edge, in lower arcs; the landing ring is about 40% of its old size.

## v5.13 (zion-sky-v482)
Lunar Rover: a soft, rounded mission boundary. The edge was the terrain square itself, and Cernan grabbed the wheel the instant you came within 40 m of it, then spun the heading and velocity together with no warning. That felt like a kick because it bypassed the tires. The mission area is now an uneven, rounded loop inside the square, at least 45 m beyond every site and stop, drawn as a dashed line on the map. Heading out, a Mission Control notice counts down about 5 seconds first ("Cernan takes the wheel in 3 s"); turn back and it clears. If you keep going, he drives with the normal controls: brakes in a straight line (a rover turning hard at speed on the Moon just slides), then turns round at walking pace and hands back once you're heading in. The takeover point moves out with speed so he has room to stop. The terrain's own end now lets you slide along it instead of cutting your speed to 30%.

## v5.12 (zion-sky-v481)
Lunar Rover: no more flips from a rim. A fast hit could reach 7 rad/s of tumble, several somersaults in one long lunar jump, from three things: the wheels' bump stops and the frame's contact with the ground had no upper limit, so a hard hit at speed gave a huge kick; the tip-over push added in v5.11 could also act on a nose-up hit at speed; and nothing soaked up a spin. Now each strut gives way past 9 kN and the frame's contact is softer and capped, the tip-over push acts only on a slow, settling tip, and tumbling faster than about 2.5 rad/s is damped. At 13 to 15 m/s into Shorty's rim the worst tumble fell from 7.4 to under 4 rad/s, a rough landing rather than a flip. Steering: the turn assist is gentler and the minimum lock smaller (full lock at 6 m/s now turns about 29° a second instead of 40), so the low grip shows again.

## v5.11 (zion-sky-v480)
Lunar Rover: crashes and rollovers. The astronauts now reach for the ground from the first instant of a crash, a quick firm reach with both arms (it had been too weak to see: forearms pointed down 0.10 on average, now about 0.5). The chassis deck is solid to the crew and loose parts, which had passed through it. In a slow rollover the seat belts hold and the crew go over with the rover, ending up under it instead of thrown clear; a hard crash still tears them loose. On its side the rover no longer bounces back up: the wheel springs were still pushing while it lay on its side, flinging it upright; they now give no support past about 70°, and beyond that tipping point it goes over. Steering: at speed the wheels had been limited by grip to about 2° (they hardly turned, and steering felt dead); they now always have at least 8° of lock, and a turn assist makes the rover's heading follow the wheels while the tires bear on the ground (about 40°/s at full lock at 6 m/s).

## v5.09 (zion-sky-v478)
Lunar Rover: rocks without gaps, and fewer, gentler ones at Shorty. Every rock had been an icosahedron reshaped corner by corner, but three.js gives each triangle its own corners, so the triangles pulled apart and showed gaps; the corners are joined first now, and the rocks come in three angular shapes cut by flat fracture planes, as fresh lunar blocks are. Shorty's floor has about 90 blocks instead of 300, and every rock's bump under the wheels is a rounded rise a little wider than the rock instead of a dome with a vertical edge, which threw the rover about. Mars Rover: lighter on phones and tablets. The Medium tier (the automatic choice on touch screens) drops antialiasing, which is costly on their dense screens, and draws at 1.25× instead of 1.5× with fewer rocks and no dust devils; the resolution can fall to 0.65× and reacts within about a second when frames run slow.

## v5.08 (zion-sky-v477)
Lunar Rover: the rag dolls' joints bend only as a body does. Elbows and knees are hinges, from straight to about 145° and 140°, one way only, with a little give sideways; hips from about 20° behind to 120° forward. Each is a hard stop, applied again after every physics step, so even a violent tumble stays within 2° of the limits (a soft spring had let elbows fold 150° backward). Thrown or rolling, each astronaut instinctively reaches for the ground with both arms to catch himself. At a discovery, Harrison Schmitt climbs out, lopes a few steps from the rover, takes the sample with the long-handled scoop the Apollo crews used (so they need not kneel in the stiff suits) and climbs back aboard; the card opens as the sample comes up, and the rover is held still meanwhile. The 76535 card shows the sample itself in 3D, turning, from NASA Astromaterials 3D's photogrammetry scan of 76535,0 (public domain; drag to turn it). Shorty matches the Station 4 photographs more closely: its floor and lower walls heaped with angular blocks, a big boulder on the rim with smaller blocks round it, and the orange soil one compact, mottled rust-orange patch beside it, a few meters long, instead of broad patches along the rim.

## v4.91 (zion-sky-v460)
Mars Rover: the landing ground is now the real Jezero seen from orbit. Its heights were already HiRISE, but its look was invented, and so was some of its shape: a stepped crater wall round the drivable circle (the terraces seen from the sky crane) and six mesas that do not exist at the landing site. Entry: the regional map is drawn inside the globe's shader, so it shares the planet's light and haze instead of sitting on it as a redder disc, and the globe is tinted to the map's color round Jezero: rust, not tan. The blue sunset in 2022 now stays hidden until every other objective is done, then appears as the surprise last objective.
Lunar Rover: Shorty moved out to about 160 m west-southwest of the lander, the way the second drive went, with smaller craters cleared off its rim. The orange soil is two patches on the rim, the larger where 74220 was scooped, instead of a band round the crater: Schmitt found a narrow zone, not a ring.
