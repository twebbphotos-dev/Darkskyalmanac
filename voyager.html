Voyager 1
Launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn, Voyager 1 is now in interstellar space — the farthest anything built by people has ever gone. Its signal takes almost a full day to reach us.

The Journey
  • 5 Sep 1977
    Launch from Cape Canaveral, 16 days after its twin, Voyager 2, on a faster route to Jupiter.
  • 5 Mar 1979
    Jupiter. Found erupting volcanoes on Io — the first active volcanoes seen beyond Earth — and a faint ring.
  • 12 Nov 1980
    Saturn and Titan. A close pass of Titan revealed a thick orange haze, and Saturn’s gravity flung Voyager 1 up and out of the plane of the planets.
  • 14 Feb 1990
    The Pale Blue Dot. From 6 billion km it turned its camera home for a family portrait of the planets — Earth a single pixel in a sunbeam.
  • 17 Feb 1998
    Most distant object ever built, overtaking Pioneer 10.
  • 16 Dec 2004
    Termination shock at 94 AU, where the solar wind slows below the speed of sound.
  • 25 Aug 2012
    Interstellar space. Crossed the heliopause — the edge of the Sun’s bubble — at 121 AU.
  • Nov 2026
    One light-day from Earth. A message sent from here takes 24 hours to arrive, and the reply another 24.
  • Early 2030s
    Silence. Power is expected to run too low for any instrument, and contact will eventually be lost.
  • In 40,000 years
    Drifts within 1.6 light-years of the star Gliese 445 — still carrying its message from Earth.
The Golden Record
A 12-inch gold-plated copper record, bolted to the side of the spacecraft with a stylus and instructions for playing it. It carries 115 pictures, greetings in 55 languages, the sounds of wind, rain, whales and a heartbeat, and 90 minutes of music from Bach to Chuck Berry. The cover shows where Earth is, mapped against 14 pulsars. In the emptiness between the stars it should survive for a billion years or more.
