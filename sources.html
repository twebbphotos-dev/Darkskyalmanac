Dark Sky Almanac
Sources & Methods
Where the facts and figures in this app come from, and how its live numbers are worked out.

Calculated, then checked. Positions, rise and set times, phases and event dates are computed on your device and compared against a second, independent ephemeris.

Facts with references. Descriptions were checked against the sources below in October 2026. Astronomy keeps moving, so distances, ages and moon counts are rounded and will be revised as measurements improve.

Live Sky Calculations
Sun, Moon and planet positions, rise and set times, twilight, phases and moon positions are calculated on your device, so they work offline and for any saved location.
  • Astronomy Engine
    Open-source astronomy library by Don Cross (MIT licence). Used for the Sun, Moon and planets: positions, rise and set, twilight, phases, oppositions, conjunctions and constellation boundaries.
    github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy
  • PyEphem
    A second, independent ephemeris. Every event date and a sample of rise, set and twilight times were recomputed with it and compared.
    rhodesmill.org/pyephem
  • Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed. (1998)
    Theory behind Jupiter’s four large moons (after Lieske), Saturn’s rings and moons (after Dourneau), sidereal time and precession. Code ported from the open-source astronomia library with VSOP87 planetary series.
    github.com/commenthol/astronomia
  • JPL Solar System Dynamics
    Approximate orbital elements of the planets, used for the solar-system views and the Sky Finder.
    ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html
  • Jupiter’s Great Red Spot
    Current longitude and drift rate from Sky & Telescope; the System II rotation formula from Project Pluto.
    skyandtelescope.orgprojectpluto.com/grs_form.htm
  • Twilight definitions
    Civil, nautical and astronomical twilight as defined by the US National Weather Service; solar times spot-checked against the NOAA Solar Calculator.
    weather.gov/fsd/twilightgml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc
Weather, Clouds & Aurora
These are the only parts of the app that need a connection.
  • Open-Meteo
    Hourly cloud cover, temperature, wind and humidity. The cloud forecast blends three global models: ECMWF IFS, DWD ICON and NOAA GFS.
    open-meteo.com
  • NASA GIBS
    The live satellite loop on the Detailed Cloud Cover page: GOES-West and GOES-East infrared imagery (ABI Band 13) in 10-minute steps.
    earthdata.nasa.gov
  • Natural Earth
    State, province and country borders, coastlines, lakes and towns drawn over the satellite loop. Public domain.
    naturalearthdata.com
  • Mars Rover · Perseverance Simulator
    Terrain from HiRISE elevation of the Perseverance landing site (NASA/JPL/University of Arizona/USGS). Rover photographs NASA/JPL-Caltech, credited on each card. Scanned rocks and ground textures from Poly Haven (CC0). Built with three.js.
    uahirise.org · polyhaven.com
  • Lunar Rover · Apollo 17
    The Taurus–Littrow valley and its mountains from NASA’s terrain model of the Apollo 17 landing site (250 m grid), with the Moon’s curvature. The ground near the lander is generated, since the model is too coarse for small craters. Rover: Deutsches Museum Digital scan; lander: NASA 3D Resources. Astronauts: “Space suit” by IgorSholopov (sketchfab.com/3d-models/space-suit-bdc42075279746dab4c8b54784276a20), CC BY 4.0, modified: textures re-encoded, arms raised, shoulders broadened, backpack, oxygen purge system and chest unit added, figure bent into a seat. Lunar gravity 1.62 m/s² (NASA Moon Fact Sheet). Site and sample cards: Apollo 17 surface photographs (AS17-134-20454, AS17-137-20990 and 20992, AS17-146-22294) and laboratory photographs of samples 76535, 79115 and 74220 (S73-19456, S73-15396, S73-15171), NASA, public domain; sample facts from the Lunar Sample Compendium and NASA’s lunar sample catalog.
  • Jezero Crater from above (3D spacecraft viewer)
    Elevation and photography from USGS Analysis Ready Data: CTX stereo elevation models (20 m) and their orthoimages, from Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (NASA/JPL/MSSS/USGS). Public domain (CC0). Colored to match a true-color orbital view. Close-up detail along Perseverance’s route from HiRISE images (NASA/JPL/University of Arizona) in the same USGS archive, aligned to the CTX map. Perseverance’s drives from NASA/JPL-Caltech’s published traverse data; milestone dates from NASA mission updates; the place photographs are NASA/JPL-Caltech (with ASU, MSSS, LANL and CNES), credited on each card.
    stac.astrogeology.usgs.gov
  • US National Weather Service
    Point forecasts for locations in the United States.
    weather.gov
  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center
    Planetary K-index forecast, used for the aurora outlook.
    swpc.noaa.gov
  • BigDataCloud
    Turns saved coordinates into a place name.
    bigdatacloud.com
Events & Meteor Showers
  • Event dates
    Oppositions, conjunctions, elongations, eclipses, equinoxes and solstices were computed with Astronomy Engine and PyEphem, and are listed by the local evening or morning to look, with clock times in your time zone.
  • International Meteor Organization
    Meteor shower peak dates and zenithal hourly rates, from the IMO Meteor Shower Calendar.
    imo.net/resources/calendar

The Moon
  • Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature
    Coordinates and diameters of the seas, craters and mountain ranges on the Moon map.
    planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov
  • NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera
    Feature descriptions and ages, including the dating of Tycho.
    lroc.im-ldi.com
  • Apollo Lunar Surface Journal
    Landing sites, dates, crews and surface activities of the Apollo missions.
    nasa.gov/history/alsj
  • Moon phases and standstills
    Phase ages, new and full moon times and the 18.6-year standstill cycle are computed with Astronomy Engine and checked with PyEphem.
Deep Sky & Constellations
Distances and ages of deep-sky objects are measurements that are refined over time, so the app gives rounded figures.
  • OpenNGC
    Positions, types and magnitudes of NGC and IC objects.

  • HYG Star Database
    Star positions, magnitudes and colours for the constellation and asterism charts.
    github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database
  • IAU constellation figures
    The constellation line figures on the Constellations page, in Star ID and the Sky Finder: the figures of the IAU constellation charts drawn with Sky & Telescope. Line data from Stellarium’s modern_iau sky culture; star positions from Hipparcos.
    stellarium
  • International Astronomical Union

Dark-Sky Places
Bortle ratings on the maps are estimates drawn from light-pollution mapping and the parks’ own dark-sky reports, not on-site measurements, so each is shown as a range. Road, trail and season details change; check with the park before you go.

Photography Tools
  • The NPF rule
    Frédéric Michaud’s formula for the longest exposure before stars trail (Société Astronomique du Havre).
    sahavre.fr/wp/regle-npf-rule
  • NOAA magnetic field calculator
    Magnetic declination, so the Sky Finder’s compass points to true north.
    ngdc.noaa.gov
  • Stacking and signal-to-noise
    The square-root rule for combining exposures, as set out in the Astropy CCD data reduction guide.
    astropy.org
  • Star ID catalog
    About 83,000 stars to magnitude 9 and the named deep-sky objects, from the HYG database v4.1 (Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogues), with proper motion carried forward to 2026.
    github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database
  • Star ID plate solving
    The four-star geometric hashing method of Astrometry.net (Lang and colleagues, The Astronomical Journal, 2010), rebuilt to run on the phone with no connection.
    doi.orgastrometry.net
